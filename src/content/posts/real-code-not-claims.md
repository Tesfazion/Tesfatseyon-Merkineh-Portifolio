A portfolio is a sales page. There's nothing wrong with that — but most
portfolios are a sales page built on claims the owner could never
defend in a conversation.

I decided to make mine differently.

## Claim versus evidence

"I increase conversions by 35%" is a claim. The only way to verify it
is to ask a follow-up question the page can't answer. That's a weak
position for a freelancer to be in during the *first* call.

"Here is the repository, and here is the build it became" is evidence.
It costs me nothing to show, and it answers more questions than a
paragraph ever could.

## What I removed

- **Invented metrics.** No "95+ Lighthouse" numbers presented as a
  guarantee.
- **Client logos I don't have.** A logo wall of platforms is only
  honest if they hired you.
- **Mock screenshots.** Every project on the [work](/web) page opens
  real code or a real deployment.

## What I got back

Trust is cheaper to earn than to repair. When a client lands on a
portfolio where every claim ends in a link they can click, the first
call starts on the level ground of *what's actually true* — which is a
much better place to negotiate from.

The craft shows in the work. The work is public. Click it.
