Everyone — including me, early on — treats retention as a single number and a
single problem: *make the first three seconds good.* That gets you past the
scroll. It does not get you watched to the end.

A retention curve is a shape, and it breaks in a few predictable places.

## Drop 1: the promise gap

The steepest fall-off is almost always just after the hook resolves. The
open made a promise, the video spent eight seconds on context, and the
viewer realised the payoff was not coming.

The fix is not a faster hook. It is **honouring the promise inside the first
beat that follows it.** If you said "this took five minutes," show the five
minutes before you explain why five minutes is hard. Context after payoff,
never before.

## Drop 2: the dead middle

Short form has a structural problem: the interesting parts are the
beginning and the end, and the middle is connective tissue. Editors who pad
the middle to hit a target length lose the viewer at the exact point they
were already drifting.

Cut it ruthlessly. If a sentence exists to make the story sound complete
rather than to move it, it is the first thing to go. The [three-second
hook post](/blog/three-second-hook) covers the opening; the middle is the
same discipline applied without a deadline.

## Drop 3: the outro

The last few seconds are where a lot of watch-through gets thrown away. A
slow fade, a logo sting, or a "thanks for watching" is asking a viewer who
just got interested to leave on purpose.

End on the last idea. Put the follow prompt *under* it or on screen with it,
not after it, and let the cut happen while they are still reading.

## Reading the curve properly

One caveat, because this is where most of this advice turns to mush: I do
not have a client retainer that hands me a retention dashboard, so I am not
claiming I optimise against a curve I cannot see. What I do is cheaper and
survives contact with reality —

- watch the first ten seconds back **at normal speed**, which is how it will
  be watched
- check that every cut has a reason to exist
- leave the export, come back an hour later, and watch it cold

> The number is a symptom. The dead beat that causes it is a decision, and
> decisions are fixable.

The craft is not in the curve. It is in removing the three places the curve
breaks.
