Every client asks for a hook that stops the scroll. Here's the honest
version: the first three seconds are easy. Keeping them watching for
the next thirty is the actual job.

The hook is a **promise**. The rest of the edit has to pay it off.

## What a hook actually does

A good open does three things in three seconds:

1. It names the tension — *"this habit was killing my numbers"*
2. It rewards the stay — *"and the fix took five minutes"*
3. It previews the shape — *"here's the before, here's after, here's how"*

If any of those three is missing, viewers get a surprise the edit won't
deliver — and they leave at the exact point the promise breaks.

## Pacing isn't speed

Beginners think retention means cutting faster. It doesn't. Speed with
no shape reads as noise and people swipe past. I cut to a **rhythm**:
longer beats for context, quicker cuts at payoff moments, and a pause
before the key line so the brain catches up.

## My checklist before export

- **Captions** land on the beat, not a second late
- **Sound** carries the emotion — silence is a tool, not a gap
- **The last frame** earns a follow, not a goodbye

I don't use expensive plugins for any of this. CapCut for speed and
DaVinci for the grade. The tool is the least interesting part of the
edit — the decisions are.
