The edit is the part people see. The part that decides whether a client
sends the next batch is the twenty minutes after it, and almost nobody
charges for those twenty minutes or admits they exist.

This is my list. It isn't long, because a long list is a list you stop
following.

## Watch it on a phone, not a monitor

This is the whole trick, and everything else is detail.

A cut that feels tight on a 27-inch monitor at full brightness is often
loose on the phone the viewer is actually holding. Phones are smaller,
often dimmer, and frequently watched with one thumb while something else
is happening. The first three seconds have to survive that.

If you can only check one thing, check it on the smallest screen you
have, at half brightness, with the sound low. If the hook still lands
there, it'll land anywhere.

## Watch it with the sound off

Sound off is not an accessibility edge case, it's roughly how a good
chunk of short-form gets consumed. Captions carry the meaning, so they
have to be complete — including the part where the speaker says
something the face doesn't.

The other thing this catches is audio that was doing work the picture
wasn't. If the video makes no sense muted, the edit is leaning on a joke
you can hear but can't see.

## The first frame, alone

Scrub to zero and look at one frame. That's the poster frame, and on some
feeds it's genuinely all that shows before autoplay kicks in.

Ask two questions about it: does it look like the video is about what it
turns out to be about, and would you stop scrolling? If the answer to
either is no, the fix is usually in the first two seconds, not in the
thumbnail.

## Watch it a second time, at speed

Play the whole thing at 1.5× or 2×. You won't catch every mistake, but
this reliably surfaces the two things that matter most:

- **Dead air** — a beat that exists because you were deciding what to do
  next, rather than because the viewer needed a moment.
- **Redundant shots** — two frames saying the same thing. They feel
  necessary while you're cutting and feel like padding on playback.

## Then the boring list

- Exports match what the platform actually wants, and I checked the
  current spec rather than last year's.
- The caption file exists, not just burned-in text, if the client needs
  it.
- No personal footage, client names, or anything sensitive in the
  background of a frame I was focused elsewhere.
- Project files are versioned and the project is closed out. The
  difference between a folder named `final_v2` and a folder named
  `2026-08-28_client_acme_final` is the difference between finding
  something and archaeology.

## Why I write this down

Because the last ten percent is invisible in the timeline. It only
exists when someone watches the exported file, on a real device, like a
real viewer — and by then you are tired of the piece, which is exactly
the wrong state to be reviewing in.

The checklist isn't craft for its own sake. It's the part that decides
whether this was a one-time job or a repeat client, and nobody ever
notices the twenty minutes.
